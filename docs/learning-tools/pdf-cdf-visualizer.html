The probability density function (PDF), \(f_X(x)\), describes how probability is spread along the number line: the probability that a continuous random variable \(X\) lands in an interval is the area under \(f_X\) over that interval. The cumulative distribution function (CDF), \(F_X(x) = P(X \le x) = \int_{-\infty}^{x} f_X(t)\,dt\), gives the probability that \(X\) is at most \(x\); it is the running area under the PDF. The two plots above update together, and any probability you compute is marked in pink in both.

Pink marks the event: on the left, the area under the PDF over the event; on the right, the part of the CDF over the event, which rises from the lower dashed level to the upper one.

Remember: For a continuous random variable, \(P(X = x) = 0\) for every single value \(x\). Probability is area under the density curve, and a single point is an interval of width zero, so it has no area. As a result, endpoints never matter: \(P(a \le X \le b) = P(a < X < b)\). The density \(f_X(x)\) is not itself a probability, so it can be larger than 1.
